Health Journal In development · Android first

For families in India

Every report, prescription and medicine in your family, in one place.

Health Journal reads the reports and prescriptions already sitting in your phone's gallery, PDFs and WhatsApp. It sorts them by person, turns prescriptions into medicine reminders, and keeps test results as trends you can show the doctor.

We're building it for one person who looks after everyone: your own records, your parents' medicines, your kids' vaccinations.

Real-life moments

Made for how records actually look

Locked PDFs, misspelled names, duplicates, old prescriptions. This is where most apps get it wrong.

The lab’s PDF is password-locked

Report.pdf · lockedOpened · 14 values

Most Indian lab PDFs are. The app asks once (often a date of birth, like 21041960) and never stores the password.

The prescription says “Cetrizine 10”

Cetrizine 10Cetirizine 10 ✓

Names are checked against India’s national medicine list. When it isn’t sure, it asks “Did you mean…?” and shows the ingredients.

The same report is saved twice

PDFWhatsApp photoShown once

Values aren’t counted twice in your trends. Pages of one report stay together as one report.

A 2022 prescription turns up in the gallery

Rx · March 2022Past · no reminders

Old prescriptions are filed as history, so nobody gets alarms for a course that ended years ago. Still taking it? One tap restarts it.

“Dolo 650 — SOS, fever above 100°F”

Dolo 650 SOSOnly when needed

SOS medicines don’t ring alarms. They sit under “only when needed”, with what they’re for.

A train ticket got picked up with the reports

Ticket.pdfKeep or delete?

If something doesn’t look like a health record, the app says so and lets you decide, instead of filing it as a report.

Made for families

One person keeps track. Everyone is looked after.

  • Add your parents, spouse and children. Each person has their own records, medicines and emergency card.
  • Share with a sibling to help manage, or just to see. You decide who can change what.
  • A note to you if a dose isn't marked, so you can call and check, even from another city.
  • Big text, plain words and tablet photos, so Amma can use it on her own.

What it won't do

It records. It doesn't prescribe.

  • It shows only what your doctor prescribed. It never suggests, swaps or changes a medicine.
  • If a value can't be read clearly, it says so. It doesn't guess.
  • Medicines are checked against India's national medicine list, so a hard-to-read name is flagged instead of quietly “fixed”.
  • Before we open it to everyone: a plain-language privacy policy and a clear consent screen. Your feedback on this matters most to us.
